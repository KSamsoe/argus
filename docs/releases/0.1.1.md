# Argus 0.1.1

0.1.1 is about getting games made on Argus to players. A game can now be played by touch on a phone or a tablet, using the whole screen. It carries its own name and icon on every platform, and on the Mac it is an app a player double-clicks. Its text can carry colour, bold and italic, the player's own keys, and small pictures. Nothing in it breaks a game made on 0.1.0.

## New: touch

- **Touch controls.** `input.toml` declares buttons and sticks (`[touch.<name>]`: `kind`, `anchor`, `at`, `size`, `label`, `floating`), and actions bind them with `touch = ["jump"]`, as they bind keys. A floating stick centres itself wherever the thumb lands in its half of the screen. The engine draws the controls in the theme's colours while touch is the device in use, and a key, a click, or a gamepad hides them again. A finger that lands on no control is the mouse, so a game played by clicking works under a finger with nothing added. What a finger presses is recorded as actions, so replays, seeks, and players' reports have it. Button prompts name the touch button. Scenarios press with `t.input:touch`, and `argus input device` sends fingers. ([Gameplay](https://github.com/KSamsoe/argus/blob/v0.1.1/docs/manual/gameplay.md#touch))
- **The whole screen on a phone.** On a phone or a tablet, the web page fills a screen wider than 16:9, as a phone on its side is, and draws at the screen's own sharpness. The world shows more at the sides. The game's words and menus stay in the middle at their own shape, and the touch controls go to the screen's edges, where the thumbs are. Held upright, the game keeps its own shape across the screen's width. A run plays the same on any screen: only the picture changes.
- **Framing for thumbs.** `Camera.touch_offset` moves the camera's aim while the player plays by touch, and lets the view pass the room's edge by as much. A hero standing on a floor is then drawn above the controls instead of under a thumb. It is zero unless a game sets it.
- **Hollow plays by touch,** with a stick, Jump, Talk, and a Menu button. [Releasing](https://github.com/KSamsoe/argus/blob/v0.1.1/docs/manual/releasing.md#on-a-phone-or-a-tablet) has how to try a web build on a phone.

## New: a game's own name and icon

- **`[game]` in `argus.toml`** names the game (`title`), its `version`, and its icon (`icon`, a sprite, and `icon_frame`, which of its frames). A pixel-art frame is scaled up by whole pixels, so a 16-pixel hero makes a sharp icon. ([Releasing](https://github.com/KSamsoe/argus/blob/v0.1.1/docs/manual/releasing.md))
- **`argus build` puts them where each platform looks,** with no compiler:
  - Windows: the icon, title, and version inside the program, for Explorer and the taskbar, and the icon on the game's window.
  - Linux: the icon beside the program and on its window.
  - The web: the page's title and icon, and a manifest, so "Add to Home Screen" on a phone gives the game's name and icon.
- **A Mac build is an app,** `<Title>.app`, with the icon and the app's details, which a player double-clicks. It is signed ad hoc, as before. A copy downloaded with a browser still has to be cleared once, and the example games' README says how.
- Every template and example game has an icon. Each template's is its hero, drawn by its art script.

## New: rich text

- **Markup in a `UiText`:**
  - `[color=harm]` takes a theme colour or a `#RRGGBB`.
  - `[b]` and `[i]` make bold and italic.
  - `[size=24]` sets the size.
  - `[img=icons:3]` puts a sprite frame among the words.
  - `[glyph=jump]` shows the player's own key for an action. It's drawn outlined like a key, Space on a keyboard or A on an Xbox pad, and changes when they pick up another device.
  - `[[` writes a bracket.
- It works inside string tables and around holes. The semantic frame and screen readers get the plain words. Markup that does not read is shown as written and reported with its node. ([Presentation](https://github.com/KSamsoe/argus/blob/v0.1.1/docs/manual/presentation.md#rich-text))
- **Delve uses it:** damage in red and healing in green in its log, the kill in bold, and a menu hint that names the player's own keys.

## New: smaller things

- **Undo a named edit.** `edit.undo` takes a `tx` to take back one particular change, not just the latest. When later changes touched the same things, it is refused with error 4718, naming each change in the way with its author and note.
- **The web click carries its modifiers.** A `ctrl+left` binding works on a web page even when Ctrl was pressed while the page's own text box had the focus.
- **A screen of another shape shows more, never less.** A window wider than the game's 16:9 shows more of the world at the sides, as before. A narrower one, such as fullscreen on a 4:3 or 16:10 screen, now keeps the game's width and shows more height, instead of cutting the sides.

## Fixed

- **A gamepad's first press in a turn-based game took a turn of its own.** The press that switched the prompts to the pad counted as a turn, and the device went back to the keyboard's on the next tick. The device in use is now a state, as the pointer is.
- **Hollow's empty space is dark.** Where no room is, Hollow showed the engine's lighter grey, at one edge of the hall and anywhere a wide screen looks past a room. Its camera now has a background in the back wall's colour.

## What you might notice

None of these break a game made on 0.1.0, but they change what you see:

- **A Mac build is `<Title>.app`.** Before, it was a folder with the program and `data/` beside it. A script of your own that copies a Mac release takes the app instead.
- **`UiText` reads markup.** A text that contains `[b]`, `[i]`, `[color=...]`, `[size=...]`, `[glyph=...]`, `[img=...]`, or `[[` now formats it. Other brackets read as written (`[E] Talk` is unchanged). To show one of those literally, write `[[` for its bracket.
- **Saves and recordings from 0.1.0 load and play the same.** `Camera`'s new `touch_offset` gets its default, zero.

## Install

`install.ps1` (Windows) or `install.sh` (Linux and macOS), from the archive or with a GitHub token from this release; see [docs/install.md](https://github.com/KSamsoe/argus/blob/v0.1.1/docs/install.md). An install from 0.1.0 updates with `argus self-update`. The macOS build is cross-built on Linux and universal (Apple Silicon and Intel); [docs/mac-testing.md](https://github.com/KSamsoe/argus/blob/v0.1.1/docs/mac-testing.md) is the packet for checking it on a Mac, with `mac-expected.json`. To play the example games with nothing installed, there's `example-games-0.1.1` for Windows, Linux, or macOS. On the Mac they are now apps to double-click, with no Swarm, whose Rust plugin is built on a Mac; each archive's `README.txt` has the controls.
