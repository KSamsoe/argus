# Phase 1 checklist

Section 18 of the design document lists phase 1 as one sentence and a gate. This file breaks the sentence into pieces, attaches every constraint from elsewhere in the document that each piece has to meet, and records status, the way `phase0-checklist.md` did for phase 0. Piece 0 is not in the sentence: it is the phase-0 gate's output, and everything in phase 1 that runs gameplay in Luau sits on it.

Phase 1 sentence: *wgpu sprite renderer with instancing and atlases; windowed and offscreen modes; keyboard, mouse, gamepad input with recording; audio mixer with event log; asset importer with sidecars and watch; tilemaps with chunks and auto-tiling; kinematic character controller; camera component; sprite animation state machines with frame events; tweens; emitter pools; materials with hot reload; semantic frames and annotated screenshots; obs.diff, obs.trace, obs.why.*

Gate: *Swarm and Delve are playable by a human in a window. The agent builds a three-room slice of Hollow with tiles, a kinematic player, one-way platforms, and a following camera, screenshots it, describes what's on screen from the semantic frame, and fixes a visual bug it found that way. The player has a run, jump, and land animation with a footstep event that fires a sound.*

Carried in from phase 0 by decision: `obs.trace` (item 13 here), `code` in `run.toml` (phase 2), `argus docs --for-agent` and `argus mcp` (phase 2), tile pathfinding under `t.expect:path` (with the tilemap and navigation grid; navigation itself is phase 3), replay and clip export on test failure (phase 2).

## 0. The Luau cost model and the broadphase

**Status: done** (2026-09-18) in `argus-ecs` (`spatial.rs`, `World::spawn_with`), `argus-sim` (the `broadphase.rebuild` tick step, `SystemCtx::within/nearest/spawn_with`), `argus-luau` (native `vector` for `vec2`, `q:spawn(names)`, `q:spawn_many`, `q:within`, `q:nearest`), `argus-index` (`Source::near`), and the daemon (lazy rebuild before a `within` query between ticks). Traces in each crate's `SPEC-TRACE.md`; the before/after table is in `crates/argus-luau/SPEC-TRACE.md`. On the stress scene the tick fell from 280 ms to 49 ms and the hit pass from 176 ms to 0.14 ms; what remains is the binding's per-call cost in per-entity loops, which is the Rust promotion's job. The phase-0 gate found that Luau at 50,000 entities cost 265 ms a tick, attributed by `prof.alloc` to a table allocation per `vec2` field access (200k allocations a tick in Swarm's chase system, 750k in its hit pass) and, at spawn, to one binding call per component per entity. Constraints:

- `vec2` fields read and write as Luau's native `vector` value: no allocation, `v.x`/`v.y`, arithmetic in the VM. Tables `{x=, y=}` stay accepted on write (§7 sandboxed, §19 "Luau performance ceiling").
- Spawning many entities is one call: `q:spawn(components)` places the entity in its final archetype at once, and `q:spawn_many(n, components, fn)` loops in Rust and hands each entity to a Luau callback.
- The shared broadphase of §12: a spatial hash over the live world, rebuilt each tick, that `world.query`'s `within` predicate hits instead of scanning (§17: `world.query` over 100k entities under 2 ms), and that Luau reaches as `q:within(pos, r, components...)` and `q:nearest(pos, components...)`. Physics tiers in item 6 build on it.
- The promotion path §19 names ("promote a Luau system to Rust without changing anything around it") stays open: declared write sets are unchanged.
- Measured on the phase-0 gate's stress scene before and after; the numbers go in `docs/phase0-gate.md`'s successor table and the `argus-luau` trace.

## 5. Tilemaps with chunks and auto-tiling

**Status: done** (2026-09-22) in `crates/argus-tiles` (a `*.tilemap.toml` index with a folder of sparse chunks per layer, auto-tiling, collision, the `Tilemap` component, visible-tile queries), `argus-assets` (`[autotile.<terrain>]` rules and tile properties in the tileset's sidecar), `argus-render` (`render_with` draws tiles in the sprite pass), `argus-scene` (maps beside entity files are data, not entities), and the daemon (`scene.tiles.create`, `scene.tiles.info`, `scene.tiles.collision`, `scene.tiles.get` over tilemaps, and `edit.tiles` in transactions with per-author undo). The design names only `scene.tiles.get` and routes every write through transactions, so the write is `edit.tiles` rather than a `scene.tiles.set`. Auto-tiling has three modes: one tile, 16 tiles by edges, and 47 tiles by edges and corners. Collision combines the layers marked for it into empty, solid, and one-way cells, which item 6 reads. Trace: `crates/argus-tiles/SPEC-TRACE.md`. Measured in release at 4,096 × 4,096 tiles and 8 layers: a 64 × 64 auto-tiled brush stroke plus save is 4.8 ms worst, a screen's visible tiles 0.18 ms, a reopen 3.8 ms, and a 1,024 × 1,024 fill plus save 85 ms, which is the known cost of a big edit. Checking a map in a real scene through the command line found that the scene loader read the map's index as an entity file and refused the scene; data indexes are now skipped, and entity ids that collide with scene file names are refused. Constraints: §12 layers over a grid, auto-tiling rules in TOML, collision from tile properties, chunked storage (§4 bulk chunks from phase 0); editable through the protocol at the rect level; §17 4,096 × 4,096 tiles and 8 layers without pauses; Hollow and Outpost are the customers.

## 6. Kinematic character controller

**Status: done** (2026-09-22) in `crates/argus-physics` (the `Collider`, `KinematicBody`, and `CharacterController` components and the `physics.control`, `physics.move`, and `physics.overlap` systems), `argus-tiles` (slope tiles and the `MapStore` the daemon and the physics step share), `argus-sim` (`SystemDesc::last` for systems that run after the rest of their stage, and `SystemCtx::events_now`), `argus-luau` (`q:events(name)`), `argus-ecs` (a broadphase visit that allocates nothing), and the daemon (physics in every scene). Two tiers of three: overlap shapes report `overlap.begin` and `overlap.end`; kinematic bodies move against every Tilemap's collision and the solid bodies, with slopes, one-way tiles and platforms, moving platforms that carry, push, and crush, step-up, and no tunnelling. The controller's coyote time, jump buffering, corner correction, jump height and time, accelerations, and step height are tune fields; the jump's apex is its declared height. Physics runs last in the sim stage, after the gameplay that sets velocities, and a late-stage script reads the tick's contacts. Trace: `crates/argus-physics/SPEC-TRACE.md`. Measured in release: 1,000 characters on a slope-and-platform map at 1.1 ms a tick, 20,000 overlap circles at 3.2 ms, and nothing allocated in a scene without bodies. Building it found two older faults: every Rust system's queries copied its access set (two allocations per query), and f32 schema defaults did not survive the index's 15 digits, so `world.describe` disagreed between live and index. The daemon test caught a fault in the new controller too: a cache of action paths keyed so that `jump` read `move`. Not felt by a person: the tests pin the mechanics exactly, but nobody has played it, and the tuning panel is phase 2. Rigid bodies stay the optional phase-5 module. Constraints: §12 three tiers on one broadphase (item 0): overlap (contacts only), kinematic (against tilemap and other kinematic bodies: slopes, one-way platforms, moving platforms, step-up, coyote time, jump buffering, corner correction as tune fields), dynamic (optional Rapier module, later); collision is an event (§8 L2); "the tier worth getting exactly right".

## 7. Camera component

**Status: done** (2026-09-22) in `crates/argus-camera` (the `Camera` and `CameraShake` components and the `camera.update` system, run last in the late stage), `argus-render` (the view from the drawing camera, interpolated, with its shake offset and roll), `argus-audio` (the listener is the drawing camera), and the daemon (cameras in every scene, headless or not, with zones from every room's bounds; `obs.frame` reports the camera and the rect it showed). One component covers both configurations: with a target it follows through a dead zone, with lookahead, smoothing as a time constant, and the bounds of the room the target is in, sliding over when the target changes rooms; with pan and zoom actions a player moves it; with neither it stays on its entity, so the example games' cameras on their players are untouched. Shake is trauma raised by a `camera.shake` event or a write, shaped by a curve, decaying, from seeded noise so a replay shakes the same way. The drawing camera is the active one with the highest priority, and a change is `camera.cut`. Each camera writes the rect it shows (`view_min`, `view_max`) every tick, which item 12's semantic frame reports. Trace: `crates/argus-camera/SPEC-TRACE.md`. Checking the camera in the renderer found an item-1 fault: previous poses were replaced every frame, so only the first frame after a tick interpolated and a display faster than the sim snapped on the others; now every frame of a tick interpolates. Not judged by a person yet: the follow settings are tune fields waiting for someone to play. Constraints: §12 follow with dead zones, lookahead, room bounds, smoothing, shake as a curve with decay, all tune fields; cameras are entities; a cut between two is an event; the semantic frame reports the camera rect (§8).

## 8. Sprite animation state machines with frame events

**Status: done** (2026-09-22) in `crates/argus-anim` (the `Animator` component and `anim.update`, first in the late stage), `argus-assets` (the animator asset: a sidecar with `type = "animator"`, parsed and checked at import with `file:line`), `argus-ecs` (field reads that build no value), `argus-render` (a sprite crossfade through `Sprite.blend_frame` and `Sprite.blend`), and the daemon (animators in every scene, refreshed on every import). States play clips of the entity's sprite; transitions are field comparisons joined by `and` (`KinematicBody.grounded and abs(KinematicBody.velocity.x) > 0.5`, or `end`, `time`, `frame`, `loops`), from the state or from `[[any]]`, with a frame window (a cancel window) and a crossfade blend; events by frame fire on every frame passed and can ask the mixer for a sound. A state's fps, anticipation (how many frames the wind-up lasts), and hold are numbers or field paths, so a game points them at its own tune fields; `Animator.speed` is one. A file that stops importing keeps its last good machine playing. Trace: `crates/argus-anim/SPEC-TRACE.md`. Measured in release: 2,000 animators at 0.175 ms a tick (the §17 row is 0.3 ms); ticks where a third of them change state at once reach 0.43 ms at p95, from building the change events. The gate's animation is met in a daemon test: a physics player goes idle, fall, land, idle, run, jump, fall, land from its body's fields, and its footsteps fire `sfx/step`, which the mixer answers. Not seen by a person: the frames were checked in rendered pictures, but nobody has watched it move. Constraints: §13 Animator component from TOML: states reference clips, transitions have conditions on component fields and blend windows, events by frame are ordinary engine events a test can assert; clip rates, anticipation and hold frames are tune fields; Aseprite tags import as clips (with item 4); §17 2,000 animators under 0.3 ms per tick, "transitions are field comparisons, not script".

## 9. Tweens

**Status: done** (2026-09-22) in `crates/argus-tween` (the `Tween` component, the easing set, and `tween.update` in the late stage), `argus-sim` (`SystemCtx::declare`, so a system whose targets come from data keeps its write set true), `argus-luau` (`q:tween(e, path, {...})`), and the daemon (tweens in every scene). A tween is an entity with a `Tween` driving one field of another entity (a number, a vec2, or one axis) from a value, or from where it is, to a value over a duration through a named easing (Penner's set: quad, cubic, quart, sine, expo, back, elastic, bounce, in, out, and in-out, plus linear and step), after a delay, repeating and reversing if asked; it emits its `on_complete` event and despawns. Overshooting curves are held inside the field's range. What is animating is a `world.query`, and a replay reproduces it to the bit. `tween.update` declares each component it touches as a write, and the animator now declares its condition reads the same way, which retires an item-8 limitation. Squash-and-stretch on landing and a camera nudge run through the protocol in a daemon test. Trace: `crates/argus-tween/SPEC-TRACE.md`. Found while testing: the repeat count was named `repeat`, a Lua keyword a script could not write as a table key; it is `repeats`. Timelines above tweens stay phase 3. Constraints: §13 a tween is a component (target path, from, to, duration, easing, on-complete event), queryable, reproduced exactly by replay; timelines above tweens are phase 3.

## 10. Emitter pools

**Status: done** (2026-09-22) in `crates/argus-particles` (the `Emitter` component, the pools, and `particles.update`, last in the late stage), `argus-assets` (the effect asset: `type = "emitter"`, checked at import with `file:line`), `argus-render` (each pool drawn as one run of instances), the daemon (particles in every scene, refreshed on every import, drawn in `obs.frame` and the window), and Swarm (sparks at every kill). An effect file gives the shape, rate, burst, duration, pool size, lifetime, speed, direction, spread, gravity, drag, world or local space, and curves over life for velocity, size, rotation, and colour as control-point lists. An `Emitter` plays one at its entity's position; a burst on demand is `Emitter.burst` or a `particles.burst {emitter, count, pos}` event from any script. Particles are not entities: each emitter's pool is six arrays of numbers, advanced in one loop the compiler vectorises and closed up in birth order, and the ECS holds only the emitter. A moving emitter's particles are born along its path, not in clumps per tick. Randomness is the run's seed. Editing an effect changes it on the next import; a broken file keeps its last good version. Trace: `crates/argus-particles/SPEC-TRACE.md`. Measured in release: 250,000 live particles across 50 emitters at 0.43 ms a tick (the §17 row is 1 ms). The first version took 0.88 ms: a table lookup for the velocity curve and closing gaps in the same loop kept it from vectorising, and since the oldest die first, every tick moved every survivor. Now the curve is exact ramps, a dead front is skipped, and the gaps close separately. Not seen by a person in motion: Swarm's sparks were checked in a rendered frame. The post stack stays phase 2; additive blending waits for item 11's materials. Constraints: §13 Emitter component (shape, rate or burst, lifetime, curves over lifetime as control-point lists); particles are not entities, each emitter owns an SoA pool updated in Rust and drawn instanced; §17 250,000 live particles under 1 ms; the post stack is phase 2.

## 11. Materials with hot reload

**Status: done** (2026-09-22) in `crates/argus-material` (the sidecar parser, the shader composition, the naga check that maps errors to the author's lines, the library, and its per-sprite components), `argus-assets` (the material asset), `argus-render` (a pipeline per material, four per-sprite numbers in every instance, runs by material and atlas, each material's uniform once a frame, particles drawn with their effect's material), the daemon (`script.errors`, `asset.list`, `asset.get`, reload on every import, sim time for shaders), and Swarm (its sparks glow, drawn additively). A material is `<name>.wgsl` defining `fn material(in: MaterialInput, p: Params) -> vec4<f32>`, with a sidecar of typed parameters: `f32`, `vec2`, `vec4`, `color`, and `texture`, each with a default, a range, a doc, and optionally `from`, a field that supplies it per sprite (`Dissolve.amount`) or from a named entity once a frame (`@torch:Transform.pos`). Blend is alpha, add, or multiply. The library is `unlit`, `lit`, `palette`, `dissolve`, and `outline`, written in the same format, and a sidecar can vary one (`shader = "outline"` with other defaults). A shader that does not compile fails its import with the line and column in the author's file, from naga with no GPU needed, and `script.errors` lists it while the last good version draws. An edit reloads in place: 20.5 ms through the protocol in a debug build (the §17 row is 100 ms). Trace: `crates/argus-material/SPEC-TRACE.md`. Found while building it: every render test skipped instead of failing when the renderer could not start, so for a few edits a broken pipeline passed the whole suite; the tests now skip only when there is no adapter at all. The bigger instance costs 200k sprites 2.5 ms a frame (17.1 to 19.6 ms). `lit` has one light; 2D lighting is phase 5. Not seen by a person in motion: the library was checked in rendered frames. Constraints: §13 a material is a WGSL shader plus typed parameters declared in a sidecar; the shipped library (lit, unlit, palette, dissolve, outline); compile errors through `script.errors` with line numbers; hot reload like scripts (§17 under 100 ms).

## 12. Semantic frames and annotated screenshots

Constraints: §8 L4 `obs.frame {format: "semantic"}` (camera rect, every visible entity with screen-space bounds, HUD text, active effects, about 3 KB) and L5 `obs.frame {format: "png", annotate: true}` with entity ids and bounds overlaid; the Describe component for game-extensible frames; the same call is a screen-reader feed (§12 accessibility); Delve lives at L4.

## 13. obs.diff, obs.trace, obs.why

**Status: done** (2026-09-22) in the daemon (`history.rs`: baselines, traces, and the three methods), `argus-sim` (events say which system emitted them; observers read the world after every tick), and `argus-ecs` (row bytes for comparing and keeping, and the emitter on every event). `obs.diff` answers "what changed since I last looked": the world is kept at each frame something looked at it, and the diff lists what appeared and vanished by kind and what changed which fields, with counts and the named entities' from and to, in under 1 KB (Swarm's arena over 300 ticks: 896 bytes). `obs.trace` gives one entity's fields over a window, the frames they changed on and to what, its components coming and going, its events, and the systems that write what changed; named entities are traced from the start for the last 3,600 frames, any other from the first time it is asked for, and a gone one is found by its old id. Recording costs 0.05 ms a tick for 1,000 named entities. `obs.why` walks back from an event: the system that emitted it (every event now says, `by` in `obs.events`), then the systems that write what it reads, in run order with file:line. Trace: rows in the daemon's, `argus-sim`'s, and `argus-ecs`'s traces. Found while building it: `obs.diff` would have crashed on an entity with more than six changed fields or a component gained or lost (a JSON array indexed past its end); a test now covers it. The phase-0 decision that events plus `who_writes` were enough for that gate stands, and `obs.trace` is here as planned. Constraints: §8 L3 `obs.diff {since: frame}` (appeared, vanished, changed which fields, with counts, about 1 KB); `obs.trace {entity, frames}` one entity's component history over a window (needs a bounded history; the snapshot ring proper is phase 2); `obs.why {event}` walks declared write sets backwards from an event to list the systems that could have caused it, in stage order; the phase-0 daemon trace records that events plus `who_writes` were enough for the gate and that `obs.trace` moves here.

## Gate

**Status: the Hollow half done (2026-09-22). "Playable by a human" waits for a person to play Swarm and Delve.** The record is [phase1-gate.md](phase1-gate.md). The agent built a three-room slice of Hollow in [examples/hollow](../examples/hollow) through the CLI: tiles, a kinematic hero, one-way planks, a following camera, and a run, jump, and land animation whose footsteps play `sfx/step`. It compared annotated pictures with semantic frames, found the pit drawn as floor and grey slivers in the shaft, and fixed both with a tile transaction. Building it found five faults, all fixed: short ids in room scenes, runtime faults missing from `script.errors`, overlapping holds cutting each other short, the harness's `release` pressing, and a binary that only started with WinLibs first on PATH. Swarm opened in a window and drew. The key names the window sends are tested against all three games' bindings. Three limits are recorded, not built: art needs a script outside the protocol, rooms stream on `scene.transition` rather than on walking, and a room narrower than the view shows its neighbours.
